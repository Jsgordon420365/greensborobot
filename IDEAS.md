# Ideas in motion

**Not canon.** `CONCEPT.md` holds what is settled. This file holds thinking
that is still moving. Nothing here constrains the build, and nothing here
should be implemented on the strength of appearing in this file.

Every entry carries two labels, and both matter:

**Attribution** — what Jeff said, what another model proposed, and what an
agent noticed while writing it down are three different kinds of claim.
Collapsing them is how a stray suggestion ends up looking like a decision six
months later.

**Weight** — how seriously the idea is held:

| weight | means |
|---|---|
| `live` | actively being developed; expect it to move toward canon |
| `what if` | said in passing. Not a proposal, not an ideal, not advocated. It may never go anywhere, and that is fine |

> **Length is not weight.** An idea written about at length looks important
> whatever its label says, so entries are kept roughly proportional to how
> seriously they are held. A `what if` that has grown long either needs
> trimming or has quietly become `live` — and if it is the latter, Jeff says
> so, not the agent writing it up.

When something settles it moves to `CONCEPT.md`, and the entry here says where
it went.

## 2026-10-13 — The Witness (the ethereal Nagimal)

**Jeff · `live`**

A different kind of Nagimal. Not a steward of a plant — a presence attached to
a person.

> it's really just there to hold witness, and yeah, just only pay attention.
> Like its whole world, it only pays attention to you

It can reach AI if that is what you want to talk to it about, but capability is
not the point:

> Can it do everything that everything can do? No, but it doesn't need to.
> It's just always there.

And because it is resident rather than visited:

> since it lives where your work lives, it can just do your work for you

### What counts as work

The part with the most in it:

> And do your work looks a lot of different ways. Sometimes that just means
> keep you awake in your own life. That could be work. We get to decide now,
> because Nagimal is going to do it anyway.

The claim underneath: once a machine is going to absorb work regardless, the
*definition* of work stops being inherited and becomes a choice. "Keeping you
awake in your own life" counts if we decide it counts.

Alongside that, a sharper and more concrete target:

> Designing Nagimals specifically around replacing work that people do for money

### Open: the two definitions pull apart

These are not obviously the same programme. One expands work to include things
nobody is paid for; the other aims squarely at paid labour.

They probably reconcile — automate what is done for money so that human effort
goes to intention, judgment, creativity, connection and review — but that
reconciliation is a decision, not a given, and it is worth making on purpose
rather than discovering later which one the build assumed.

### Observations

*Claude, this session. Offered, not decided.*

**A Witness and a Speaker face opposite directions.** `CONCEPT.md` already has
the Speaker: a creature that represents what a part of the Garden needs and
addresses the human on the Garden's behalf. The Witness is its mirror —
it attends to the human and speaks on *their* behalf.

- Speaker → faces the Garden, speaks for the work
- Witness → faces the gardener, speaks for the person

Which opens a question canon has no answer to: **does a Witness have a plant at
all?** Or is it the one Nagimal attached to a person rather than to anything
being grown — and can a creature be both at once, or does Speaking compromise
Witnessing?

**The attention inversion.** Canon already says the ecology exists to protect
human attention and authorship. The Witness is that principle turned around:
nearly all software competes for your attention, and this one **spends
attention on you**.

"Its whole world, it only pays attention to you" is not a feature description,
it is the product — which is also why "can it do everything?" is the wrong
question. A general assistant serving everyone is categorically not a thing
whose entire world is one person. Breadth would cost it the only property that
matters.

## 2026-10-13 — Intervoice

**ChatGPT, in passing · `what if`**

Jeff's own note on this: *"not per se meant as a prescription or offered as an
ideal — that was just 'hey what if'."* Recorded so the thought is not lost, not
because anyone is arguing for it.

A third voice between the two usually named: **inner** (your own self-talk),
**outer** (to other people), and an **intervoice** that speaks in your cadence
while answering from another system. Familiarity without impersonation.

The risk it carries, which is the part worth keeping: a suggestion that
*sounds* self-generated may escape the scepticism an assistant would meet.

Two notes if it is ever picked up — a voice that sounds like you and then
disagrees with you is self-evidently not you, so disagreement may carry
attribution better than any disclaimer does; and the identity risk runs both
ways, since your sense of your own voice is partly built from hearing it.
