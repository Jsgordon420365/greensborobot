# Ideas in motion

**Not canon.** `CONCEPT.md` holds what is settled. This file holds thinking
that is still moving — concepts raised, roles proposed, questions open. Nothing
here constrains the build, and nothing here should be implemented on the
strength of appearing in this file.

Entries are dated and attributed, because the distinction between *what Jeff
said*, *what another model proposed*, and *what an agent noticed* matters more
here than it does in canon. When something settles, it moves to `CONCEPT.md`
and the entry here says where it went.

## 2026-10-13 — The Witness (the ethereal Nagimal)

*Raised by Jeff, thinking out loud. His phrasings kept where they carry the
idea.*

A different kind of Nagimal. Not a steward of a plant — a presence attached to
a person.

> it's really just there to hold witness, and yeah, just only pay attention.
> Like its whole world, it only pays attention to you

It can reach AI if that is what you want to talk to it about, but capability is
not the point:

> Can it do everything that everything can do? No, but it doesn't need to.
> It's just always there.

And because it is resident rather than visited:

> since it lives where your work lives, it can just do your work for you

### What counts as work

The part with the most in it:

> And do your work looks a lot of different ways. Sometimes that just means
> keep you awake in your own life. That could be work. We get to decide now,
> because Nagimal is going to do it anyway.

The claim underneath: once a machine is going to absorb work regardless, the
*definition* of work stops being inherited and becomes a choice. "Keeping you
awake in your own life" counts if we decide it counts.

Alongside that, a sharper and more concrete target:

> Designing Nagimals specifically around replacing work that people do for money

### Open: the two definitions pull apart

These are not obviously the same programme. One expands work to include things
nobody is paid for; the other aims squarely at paid labour.

They probably reconcile — automate what is done for money so that human effort
goes to intention, judgment, creativity, connection and review — but that
reconciliation is a decision, not a given, and it is worth making on purpose
rather than discovering later which one the build assumed.

## 2026-10-13 — Intervoice

*Proposed by ChatGPT in the same thread, responding to part of the exchange not
captured here. Recorded as a proposal, not as Jeff's settled position.*

A third voice, between the two usually named:

| | |
|---|---|
| **inner voice** | private self-talk; generated by your own mind |
| **intervoice** | a bridge: speaks in your voice, answers from another system |
| **outer voice** | communication with other people |

> If your inner voice speaks in your own voice, why shouldn't your Intervoice?

The proposal is **familiarity without impersonation** — not an assistant with
an engineered personality, and not a hidden part of your own mind, but an
external intelligence arriving in a cadence you already recognise.

Practical rather than purely philosophical, since a voice sample for Qwen TTS
already exists.

### The risk it names

A suggestion that *sounds* self-generated may escape the scepticism you would
apply to an assistant. It borrows the authority of your own thought without
having earned it.

ChatGPT's stated mitigation: always preserve the distinction between what you
thought and what the machine contributed, even when both sound identical.

### Proposed first experiment

Record three reflective questions. Have the system answer, synthesised in your
voice. Compare against the same answers in a neutral synthetic voice.

The question is not which sounds more realistic. It is **which helps you think
more clearly, and whether either makes you less likely to question the
response.**

## Observations

*Noticed by Claude (this session) while recording the above. Offered, not
decided.*

### The Witness and the Speaker face opposite directions

`CONCEPT.md` already has the **Speaker**: a creature that represents what a
part of the Garden needs, and addresses the human on the Garden's behalf.

The Witness is its mirror. It attends to the human and addresses the human on
*their own* behalf.

- Speaker → faces the Garden, speaks for the work
- Witness → faces the gardener, speaks for the person

Which raises a structural question the canon has no answer to yet: **does a
Witness have a plant at all?** Or is it the one Nagimal attached to a person
rather than to anything being grown — and if so, can a creature be both at
once, or does Speaking compromise Witnessing?

### The attention inversion

The canon already says the ecology exists to protect *human attention and
authorship*. The Witness is the same principle turned around: nearly all
software competes for your attention, and this is a thing that **spends
attention on you**.

"Its whole world, it only pays attention to you" is not a feature description.
It is the product. Which also explains why "can it do everything?" is the wrong
question — a general assistant serving everyone is categorically not a thing
whose entire world is one person. Breadth would cost it the only property that
matters.

This also sits well with the substrate decision: a Witness lives where your
work lives. It is not somewhere you go.

### Disagreement may be the honest attribution signal

ChatGPT raised that the voice could sound like you without always agreeing with
you. That may be stronger than it was given credit for.

A disclaimer is a label you stop reading by the third day. But a voice that
sounds exactly like you and then **contradicts** you is self-evidently not you
— the attribution is carried by the content rather than bolted on beside it.
Disagreement would be doing structural work, not just being a nice feature.

### A second-order risk, not yet named

The stated risk runs one way: mistaking the machine for yourself.

There is a slower one running the other way. Your sense of your own voice is
partly built from hearing it. A system that speaks in your cadence, often, over
a long time, is contributing to the reference you compare yourself against —
not just "did I think that?" in the moment, but what you take your own voice to
sound like at all.

Worth watching over months rather than per-utterance. It does not argue against
the idea; it argues for noticing early.
